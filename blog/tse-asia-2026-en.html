When does a security alert become actionable? Takeaways from The Security Event Asia 2026

Áron Tarkó represented Tarkó Investigations at The Security Event Asia, held from 10 to 12 November 2026 at the Sands Expo & Convention Centre in Singapore. The conference and exhibition brought together corporate security professionals, technology providers and training organisations. For us, it offered a chance to explore how security teams move from a system alert to verified information they can act on.

What was on the agenda, and who took part?

The security exhibition ran alongside events focused on fire safety, workplace health and safety, emergency services and systems integration. Temasek Polytechnic’s Security Industry Institute brought skills and professional development into the conference programme. ASIS International APAC, its Singapore Chapter and the OSAC Singapore Chapter supported the Security Exchange. The programme also featured Matt Butterworth, Head of Physical Security at the UK Foreign, Commonwealth & Development Office.

On the exhibition floor, ICT showcased its Protege GX, Protege WX and Access+ systems. Commend brought a different perspective with Ivy Live Translation, which supports real-time communication between people who speak different languages.

What we took away

An access-control log can tell you when a credential was used. To understand what happened, an investigator needs to establish who held the credential, what access permissions applied at the time and which independent records corroborate the sequence of events. The same discipline applies to due diligence. A public company record is a starting point, and its relevance depends on how current it is and what other sources corroborate it.
